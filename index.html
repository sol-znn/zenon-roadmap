What Phase 1 is

In November 2022 the Zenon team described Phase 1 in three layers.1

Foundation
A faster, safer core, on-chain governance, smart contracts and audits.
Access
Bridges, Bitcoin swaps, wallets and exchanges.
Adoption
Hardware wallets, an NFT standard and marketing.

No date was set: Phase 1 depends on what gets built.1 Mr Kaine later named five milestones: new consensus, dynamic plasma, interoperability, zero-knowledge fast sync and new networking.2

Where things stand

  • Latest release: go-zenon v0.0.8, April 2025.3
  • Upgrades cannot be switched on: the community key expired and the original key's holder is unknown.4,5
  • Atomic swaps and the bridge to Ethereum and BNB Chain are live.
  • Dynamic plasma and new networking are merged on the test branch, not on mainnet.3
  • The chain is quiet: most momentums are empty.

The plan, in order

Four stages. Effort is in engineer-months (EM): one experienced developer for a month, audits included.

  1. 1 Start now

    Needs no network upgrade.

    • Clean releases

      Reproducible builds with vulnerability checks.

      1 to 2

    • Bitcoin swaps

      Swap BTC for ZNN or any token with the HTLCs already on mainnet.

      2 to 3

    • An identity for Sentinels

      Each Sentinel publishes a key and what it serves. The peg will rely on it.

      part of 6 to 10

    • Smart contracts on a rollup

      An EVM rollup that reads its transactions from mainnet. The base layer does not change.

      4 to 6

    • Fast sync

      New nodes start from a verified snapshot in minutes.

      4 to 6

  2. 2 Unlock upgrades

    Every later stage needs this.

    • Governance and multisig

      Pillars vote upgrades on, and a multisig replaces single admin keys. Fastest route: use the community key once to switch governance on, then retire it.4 Fallback: a hard fork.5

      4 to 6

  3. 3 First upgrades

    Each one needs a Pillar vote.

    • Dynamic plasma

      Lifts the cap of 100 transactions per momentum and prices data and computation separately.

      5 to 8

    • New networking

      libp2p, for better peer discovery.

      2 to 4

    • Fast finality and light wallets

      Pillars sign each momentum, making it final in seconds. A state summary lets light wallets ("Sentries") verify it.20 It comes before the peg: on a test network, a long split never healed without it.

      10 to 16

    • zBTC peg

      Bitcoin held by a FROST threshold key shared across Pillars.15 A Bitcoin light client in the node checks every deposit. It starts small, under a cap.

      8 to 13

    • Bitcoin checkpoints

      Momentum hashes written to Bitcoin by the peg's transactions, so history cannot be rewritten.12

      2 to 4

    • A paid job for Sentinels

      Sentinels serve archives and snapshots, relay Bitcoin for the peg, and are paid for it.19 They hold no peg keys.

      6 to 10

  4. 4 Later, when needed

    Each starts when demand justifies it.

    • Proof-checked rollup

      The base layer checks rollup proofs, so withdrawals need no committee.

      14 to 24

    • Quantum-safe accounts

      A new signature type, ML-DSA,21 with a deadline for moving old keys.22

      8 to 12

    • New consensus

      All Pillars propose blocks at once, with sub-second confirmation. Decided in late 2028.

      30 to 50

    • Zero-knowledge sync

      Phones check the whole chain from one proof.

      12 to 20

About 110 to 185 engineer-months, plus 15 to 30 for wallets, hardware signing and a DEX through Accelerator-Z. Three small teams can land most of it by mid-2029.

What we would change

The 2020 whitepaper23 and Mr Kaine's guidance mostly still hold. Four mechanisms now have better options.

OriginalInsteadWhy

  • Original: Narwhal and Tusk consensus

    Instead: Mysticeti, or Mahi-Mahi to stay fully asynchronous

    Why: Sui moved from Narwhal to Mysticeti in 2024,6 and IOTA followed in 2025.7 Mahi-Mahi is Tusk's asynchronous successor.8

  • Original: RandomX proof of work

    Instead: Equi-X

    Why: RandomX takes up to 15 milliseconds and 256 MB to check a hash.9 Equi-X takes about 50 microseconds and 2 MB, and has protected Tor since 2023.10

  • Original: Extension chains run by Pillars

    Instead: Rollups checked by the base layer

    Why: The Cosmos Hub is dropping shared security after chains left and revenue did not arrive.11

  • Original: Merge-mined proof of work for security

    Instead: Timestamps on Bitcoin

    Why: They stop long-range attacks without paying miners.12

Three more choices follow. Finality should be fast, as Solana voted for in 2025.13 Privacy should be opt-in on the rollup, because EU exchanges may not offer privacy coins from July 2027.14 The zBTC vault should use FROST, not GG20, whose flaw cost THORChain $10.7 million in May 2026.16

Schedule

Targets, not promises. Everything after governance waits for a Pillar vote.

Decision points

  • About 1 Nov 2026

    Are most Pillars running v0.0.9 when the upgrade window opens?

    Yes: governance goes live around mid-2027.

    No: a hard fork, about six months later.

  • May 2027

    Do dynamic plasma and libp2p run cleanly on testnet for eight weeks?

    Yes: Pillars vote to switch them on.

    No: fix and test again.

  • Dec 2027

    Has a BitVM2 bridge (one honest operator is enough) run a year without loss?17

    Yes: once finality is live, plan to move a large reserve to it.

    No: keep the Pillar vault under its cap.

  • Each quarter

    Has the Lightning Network shipped PTLCs?18

    Yes: add a Lightning leg to the private swap.

    No: nothing waits.

  • Dec 2028

    Is mainnet busy (over about 50 transactions per momentum), or does an app need sub-second finality?

    Yes: build the new consensus.

    No: check again in a year.
